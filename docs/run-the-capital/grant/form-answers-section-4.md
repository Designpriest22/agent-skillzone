# Grant form, Section 4: Delivery capacity & impact

## Revenue model summary
Run The Capital is free to play. It earns from three sources: in-game brand placements (billboards and sponsored stages, already tested with live event ads for Odumodu Blvck and Wizkid), event tie-ins and sponsored tournaments linked to TCM’s festival calendar, and optional cosmetic items priced ₦350–₦800, paid through Paystack and never pay-to-win. Alcohol brands stay at physical events only. Projected revenue is ₦12.2M in year 1, ₦36M in year 2 and ₦68M in year 3, reaching break-even in year 2 against about ₦30M yearly running costs.

## Track record summary
The Capital Movement (TCM) has built Abuja’s live culture since 2016. Over ten years we have created 100+ events and campaigns, moved 50,000+ people and worked with 20+ brands. We run our own properties (TCM Festival since 2016, Meat & Greet with 16+ editions, OTB by TCM) and deliver partner productions such as Capital Block Party and Festival of Lights. Our Odumodu Blvck takeover of Capital Block Party sold out. For this project, a working build of the game already exists: Odumodu Blvck as the lead, 6 playable stages, player profiles, leaderboards, secure payments and live player analytics.

## Skills development outcomes
60 young Abuja creatives trained through the Capital Game Lab, in two cohorts (15, then 45). Trainees learn game art, character animation, sound design and game coding by working on real stages of Run The Capital. Each graduate leaves with a portfolio of shipped work and a certificate, and the strongest are offered paid contracts on later seasons of the game. The 18 contracted creatives also gain experience shipping a commercial game, building a games production skill base in Abuja.

## Cultural & social benefits
- Puts Abuja on the world’s screens: real streets, neighbourhoods and artists reach players across Nigeria and the diaspora (target: 130,000 players in 12 months).
- Starts a home-grown games industry in the capital, beyond Lagos.
- Gives young people paid work, digital skills and a free, data-light game that celebrates their own city.
- Gives Abuja artists a new digital stage to reach fans between shows.
- Presents Abuja as a home of culture and creativity, not only government.
