04 — Strategic diagnosis

The problem isn’t the design. It’s that every edition starts from zero.

Each flyer is judged alone, so brand equity, audience memory and buying intent never compound. In December, when every weekend in Abuja is fighting for the same people, that is expensive.

01 · Recognition

No system

Nothing carries from Calabar to Ikom to Abuja, so followers don’t recognise a new edition in the feed.

02 · Desire

Selling a flyer, not a night

Posts announce facts; few show what it feels like to be there — the food, the crowd, the people.

03 · Conversion

No path to purchase

Abuja creative lacks venue, price and a single link. Interest has nowhere to go.

The plate-record (music on a dinner plate), halftone duotone portraits, menu leaders, the receipt and the rubber stamp.

Photography. Direct-flash, documentary: steam, hands, plates, faces mid-laugh. Graded warm (pepper) into violet shadows. No stock, no staged smiles.

Artists. Real press photos, cut out and treated as halftone duotone — one look for every name on the bill, whatever the source quality.

Motion. Records spin up, receipts print line by line, stamps slam on the beat, menu leaders draw across. 6–15s loops cut to music.

18 — Conversion & FOMO mechanics

Give people a reason to buy today, not on the day.

Price steps

Published deadlines (“Early orders close [date]”) on every ticket post. Dates set by client.

Kitchen closed

Stamp each sold-out tier publicly — scarcity people can see.

Table captains

One person orders for the group; we give them the share assets to round up the squad.

Pay-day window

Heaviest ticket push 25–30 Nov, as end-of-month salaries land.
