The brief

What the identity has to carry

Destination Nigeria brings public institutions, states, businesses, communities and creative partners together around one visitor journey. The identity is the thing they all sign.

One platform, many owners

Federal and state institutions, operators, communities and creative partners will all put their names to the same work. The mark has to sit comfortably beside a state seal, an airline logo and a community co-operative.

Honest about safety

The concept note is clear that the campaign should not claim every destination is risk-free. The identity should feel calm and credible: the voice of verified information, not a hard sell.

Practical before promotional

Promotion only works if the visitor journey works. The brand has to live on booking pages, arrival signage, training certificates and feedback forms as easily as on a campaign film.

Every region, one country

A national brand cannot belong to one region, one faith or one ethnic group. Each concept is tested against that, and its watch-outs say where it leans.

Four concepts

Four ideas, each found only in Nigeria

Every concept starts from a place, a craft or an object that exists nowhere else. All four work in one colour and hold up at favicon size, and they are finished to the same level so they can be judged on the idea.

01

Lokoja, Kogi State · 7.80° N, 6.74° E

Confluence

Where the Niger meets the Benue

At Lokoja the country's two great rivers meet and run on side by side, each holding its own colour for a long stretch before they mix. The mark draws that moment: two currents arrive from opposite banks and travel on together. It is the platform's job in one shape. Federal and state, public and private, host and visitor move the same way without losing who they are.

It reads as

  • Two rivers joining at the confluence
  • A figure with arms raised in welcome
  • A fork in the road, where a journey starts

Strengths

  • Rooted at the geographic centre of the country, where north and south meet.
  • The simplest silhouette of the four, clear at 16 px.
  • The two bands give co-branding a natural logic: one current is the platform, the other the partner.

Watch-outs

  • Raised-arms figures are common in charity and sport marks, so launch communications should lead with the rivers.
  • The Y echoes the wavy band on the national coat of arms. That helps recognition, and it needs a clearance check with the federal office responsible for national symbols.
02

Kano city walls · 12.00° N, 8.52° E

Ƙofa

The gate where travellers were received

Ƙofa is Hausa for gate. The old walls of Kano were pierced by more than a dozen of them, and the gate was where a traveller was met and welcomed into the city. The mark is a tapering mud wall crowned with zanko pinnacles, a parabolic doorway, and the sun resting on the threshold. It puts arrival, the moment the concept note most wants to improve, at the centre of the brand.

It reads as

  • A city gate, open to the visitor
  • A building: hotels, venues, investment
  • Sunrise seen through a doorway

Strengths

  • Speaks directly to arrival and welcome, the heart of the visitor journey.
  • Architecture links naturally to investment, hospitality and infrastructure.
  • The laterite palette stands apart from the green most national marks default to.

Watch-outs

  • Zanko and mud walls are Hausa architecture, so audiences in the south and east may read the mark as northern rather than national.
  • Arched doorways can carry religious associations. The parabola is kept plain for that reason, and it should still be tested with audiences.
03

Iseyin · Akwete · Okene · Bida

Aso-oke

Many strips, one cloth

Across Nigeria, cloth is woven on narrow looms in long strips and then sewn edge to edge: aso-oke in Iseyin, akwete in Abia, Ebira weaving in Okene, Nupe weaving in Bida. Each strip is finished on its own, and the cloth only exists once they are joined. The mark is that cloth, lifting like a flag, with two gold weft threads running through every strip.

It reads as

  • Hand-woven strip cloth
  • A flag lifting in the wind
  • A folded travel map

Strengths

  • The strongest system of the four: every state can have its own strip, and the 36 states and the FCT make one cloth.
  • The folded-map reading ties it to discovery and trip planning.
  • Puts the creative economy, one of the platform's objectives, at the front.

Watch-outs

  • Seven strips need a simpler three-strip version below 32 px.
  • Aso-oke is the best-known name and it is Yoruba, so the launch story must name the weaving traditions of other regions too.
  • More colours to control in print and on partner materials.
04

Nok, Kaduna State · c. 1500 BCE to 1 CE

Nok

See it for yourself

The terracotta heads of the Nok culture are among the oldest figurative sculpture in sub-Saharan Africa, and their eyes are unmistakable: a flat upper lid, a full lower lid and a pupil pierced right through the clay. The mark takes that eye and sets it under a crescent brow. It invites visitors to come and look, and it promises that what they see is what they will find.

It reads as

  • The eye of a Nok terracotta head
  • The sun sinking below the horizon
  • A calabash bowl, offered in welcome

Strengths

  • Heritage of world standing, known to museums and scholars everywhere.
  • The most ownable silhouette of the four.
  • "See it for yourself" backs the campaign's promise of verified experiences.

Watch-outs

  • A single large eye can feel watchful, so it should not be used very large or alone on security-related material.
  • Nok terracottas are at the centre of anti-looting work. Telling the heritage story with the national museums will keep the brand on the right side of it.
  • The serif wordmark has no Nigerian-language characters, so Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo headlines must use the display face.

Guide · Logo

The logo suite

Use the horizontal lockup by default. Use the stacked version where space is square or tall, and the mark alone only where Destination Nigeria is already named nearby: app icons, social avatars, pattern sign-offs.

Clear space and minimum size

xxxx

Keep clear space of x on every side, where x is a quarter of the mark's height. Nothing enters it, including state seals, partner logos and the edge of the page.

Don't

Guide · Pattern

The cloth: 37 strips, one country

The supporting pattern is borrowed from concept 03 whichever mark is chosen. There is one strip for each of the 36 states and one for the Federal Capital Territory, each with its own base tone and weft placement.

Edges, not backgroundsRun the cloth along the top or bottom of a layout, or down a spine. Never put text on it.

A strip for each stateA partner state uses its own strip as the accent on co-branded material, so every state's work is recognisably part of the whole.

Crop, don't redrawCrop the master cloth to the space you have. Don't reorder, recolour or stretch individual strips.

Guide · Typography

Type that can spell Nigeria's languages

Both main faces carry the full Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo character sets, including hooked letters, underdots and tone marks. Many popular typefaces do not, and the brand should never have to choose between a font and a correct spelling. All three are free under the SIL Open Font License.

Guide · Voice

Warm, specific, straight

The brand speaks like a well-informed host: glad you are coming, clear about what to expect.

Say what is verified

Name the place, the date and the operator. If something is not confirmed, say so.

Be straight about risk

Give visitors what they need to make informed choices. Never claim a destination is risk-free.

Welcome in every language

Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Pidgin and English all belong, spelled with their proper characters and tone marks.

Show the work

Point to commitments, owners and results. The 12-month action agenda is part of the story.

Avoid

Nigeria is 100% safe. Just come!

Say

Verified experiences, with current travel guidance from host states and operators.

Avoid

Unlock limitless opportunities in the giant of Africa.

Say

Investment-ready projects from host states, each with a summary and a named contact.

Avoid

Amazing deals!!! Book now before it's too late.

Say

Packages from partner hotels, airlines and tour operators, with dates, terms and booking links.

Guide · Imagery

Real places, real hosts, permission on file

The concept note asks for a media toolkit with verified information and image permissions. These rules make that the house style.

Name the place

Every image carries its location and state. If it can't be named, it isn't used.

Hosts, not props

Show people as guides, cooks, artists and operators, named where they agree to it.

Permission on file

Release and consent recorded for every image. Community shoots also need a safety plan and a local benefit record.

Light as it is

Natural light and honest colour. No heavy filters, no skies swapped in.

Leave out

Safari clichés borrowed from elsewhere, poverty imagery, and crowds staged for the camera.
